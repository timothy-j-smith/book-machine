## SPINE (Architecture)

The surface plot is the match against Raja Casablanca Academy.

The spine is **the inheritance of the father's interrupted life, monumentalized by the Hassan II Mosque sitting at the family's doorstep.** Ahmed Benali was once called The Phantom. His football life ended for reasons not fully spoken. His son receives Nike PHANTOM ELITE boots and *attempts* the goal his father never scored.

**Deepest articulation:** What gets inherited is not the winning. It is the *attempting* — the willingness to step onto the pitch when the game is already rigged. The boy scored the goal of his life and the linesman's flag disallowed it. The country took the father's career and gave nothing back. The country takes the son's goal and gives him offside. Same mechanism. Same family. (Source: lived parental witness — honor without surfacing.)

**Placed at three structural beats:**
1. **Birthday breakfast** — Ahmed's glance toward the kitchen window where the mosque stands.
2. **Vespa ride** — *The mosque was, for a moment, his father's hands on his shoulder.*
3. **Courtyard ending** — *"They finished it the year I was born." ... "Great hope watching over us." ... "But we cracked."*

## THE TWO GRAFFITI WALLS (architectural axis)

The book opens its practice chapter under a freshly painted Raja declaration and closes its final scene under a faded Wydad declaration. Both walls are in their original languages and *neither is translated in the prose.*

- **Chapter 5 (Weight):** *F'BLADI DELMOUNI* — green and white. Raja Casablanca's wall. ("In my country, they oppressed me.") Painted fresh.
- **Chapter 7 (Phantom):** *MI ALMA VA A GANAR* — red and white. Wydad's wall. ("My soul is going to win.") Faded into brick.

The Wydad-Raja rivalry is in the book without any line of prose having to name it. Two clubs, two walls, one family. The book has eaten the rivalry whole.

**Critical convention for future revision:** Do not translate either graffiti in the prose. Trust the architecture. The reader who can read Darija or Spanish gets the second layer; the reader who cannot absorbs the visual fact and the surrounding context.

## CHARACTERS

Names are locked. They have become real people and cannot be swapped.

- **Youssef Benali** (11→12). POV. Close third. Watches cats. Inherits The Phantom's gift.
- **Ahmed Benali**, *The Phantom.* Father. Pours cement for the Grand Stade Hassan II. Once a street-football prodigy. Cement-dust hands.
- **Fatima.** Mother. Present in four key gestures across the book: setting tea, brushing lint off the boy's shoulder, the tagine simmering, *the way his mother placed bread on the cloth at dinner* (in the lifted-ball metaphor). The bread-on-the-cloth line is the deepest thing the book says about her — she is the *grammar* of the most important moment in the book. Not under-developed. Everywhere, in the way a kitchen is everywhere.
- **Aicha.** Younger sister. *Aicha's eyes were Fatima's eyes — black, the same lift at the outer corner.* Hands Youssef the bottle-cap crown. Cut from the match equalizer scene; the crown lives in the reader's memory from chapter three without needing to be re-staged.
- **Omar.** Best friend. *Nodded once, the way a person agrees to something that is not a joke and not a promise but heavier than either.*
- **Khalid.** Omar's older brother. The Vespa. The phone.
- **Karim.** Coastal boy. Long graceful strides.
- **Badr.** Raja Casablanca Academy captain. *His eyes went to Youssef's toes.* The witness who survives the linesman: after the disallowed goal he tells Youssef, *"It counted. It counted."*
- **Hajj Brahim.** Tells the story of The Phantom at the birthday.
- **The French tourist.** Delivers the Nike box. *Pour le chat magique.*
- **The gardien.** Silent custodian. *When he crossed his arms the back of his shirt wrinkled into a perfect Y.*
- **Isklander, Chams-Eddine, Yazid, Mohammed.** Medina boys' team. Yazid (the boy who *never quit,* with electrical tape on his separating sole) scores the equalizer — *Someone who had learned to score within spaces where there is no room for a backswing.*

### Cats
- **Simo.** Gray, mismatched eyes (one copper, one gray). Opens and closes the book.
- **Hamza.** Scarred orange tabby. *A stillness that bordered on contempt.*
- **Lalla.** Black female. Still as a doorway, then sudden.

## VOCABULARY SYSTEM — Toes, Soles, Feet

Three-tier foot-vocabulary system. Future professors must preserve this distinction.

### Toes — inheritance, artistry, the gift
Reserved word. The most charged moments.

### Soles — contact, conditioning, wear
- *the soles cracked and hard from years of stone*
- *the energy returning through the soles, through the individual cleats*

### Feet — the general organ, the outside view
- *Your feet. Your movement, it's the cobblestones.* (Karim)
- *Badr's first look:* *Youssef's bare feet*
- *fathers' dreams and sons' feet*

Outsiders see *feet.* The inheritance lives in *toes.* The labor lives in *soles.*

## REMAINING SCALPEL (small, before the typo pass)

1. **Trim the Raja-wall framing in Chapter 5.** Current: *and struck the surrounding concrete wall where the words, F'BLADI DELMOUNI, were freshly painted green and white.* Suggested: *Isklander's overhit pass struck the surrounding wall, green and white paint still wet: F'BLADI DELMOUNI.* (Let the wall arrive as a fact, not as a thing the prose presents — mirrors how the Wydad wall arrives at the end.)
2. **Drop *his mother* before Fatima's first appearance.** Try: *In the apartment, Fatima set two glasses of mint tea.* Or drop the name: *In the apartment, his mother set two glasses of mint tea.*
3. **Singular shoulder.** *Fatima brushed a piece of lint off his shoulder.* (Cut *bare,* which repeats *barefoot* earlier in the chapter.)
4. **Trim Aicha at the birthday.** Consider cutting *When she smiled, the skin just above her eyebrows softened.* The eye-inheritance line is doing heavy work alone. The bottle-cap crown sentence following is hose. The middle sentence may be one detail too many.
5. **Cut Aicha from the match equalizer.** Remove *Aicha rose, the bottle-cap crown in her left hand.* Let the chapter-three image live in the reader's memory without being re-staged.
6. *Whatever he was going to say, he kept behind his teeth.* — Badr exchange after the whistle. Slightly over-narrates what the previous lines already showed. Try just: *He smiled a goodbye and turned toward the academy bus.*
7. *He thought for a moment longer, looking back toward the city.* — Youssef-Badr exchange. Try: *Youssef said, "Rue de la Douane." He looked back toward the city. "Qitat al-Medina."*
8. *Youssef rubbed his fingertips across his eyebrows.* — body gesture trying to do emotional work that the surrounding prose is already doing. Try cutting.
9. *Lost in thought.* — Vespa ride home. The only general phrase in a paragraph of specifics. Cut.
