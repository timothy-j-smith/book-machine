## 2. How each chapter gets drafted

The same five steps for every chapter:

1. **Before writing,** the Professor puts that chapter's beat list in chat, one screen at most, pulled from the log and its card. Student T changes, cuts or adds to it. If a parked detail needs deciding first, such as Younes's move, it is asked about one question at a time.
2. **The draft.** Whoever holds the pen writes it, and the pen can change hands chapter by chapter. When the Professor drafts, he leaves the door open rather than tidying every beat.
3. **See What I See.** Every draft or edit comes with what was done and why, including any line that fails the voice-age test.
4. **Student T decides what stays.** Only then does it go into `chapters/`.
5. **Back office.** The Professor updates the story map, who-knows-what and the story state after each chapter. Student T does not track any of it.

**Renumbering** happens once, when "Hand" is saved. Weight, Sparrow and Phantom shift up one, and the chapter headings and the map are updated at the same time.

**Gauging length.** The word count is reported after each chapter. The plan as designed lands around 10,000–12,000 words, at the lower end of the long-story range. If chapters come out fuller because the material asks for it, especially Hand and Phantom, the novella question reopens on evidence rather than ambition. We'll know after the first three chapters.

**Who holds the pen for Sparrow:** to be decided at the start of drafting. Either Student T drafts and the Professor edits, or the Professor drafts and Student T edits.
