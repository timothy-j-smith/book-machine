# Cold read: Qitat al-Medina, chapters 1 to 7

Date: 2026-10-23
Kind: Cold read, straight
Read: `title.md` and chapters 1 to 7 in order, in full. Nothing else: no back office, no `garage/`, no feedback. The folder was named `dump/` on the day of the read. It is `garage/` now.

---

## 1. What this story is about

An eleven-year-old boy in the Casablanca medina learns football from the street cats. He brings together a team of medina and beach boys and nearly beats the Raja academy, but his winning goal is ruled out. Underneath, it's about inheritance: a son carrying his father's buried legend, and a place teaching a body something no academy can.

## 2. What works

**The cats as teachers, start to finish.** Chapter 1 sets the idea up exactly: "the shortest distance between two points was not a line but a hesitation." It pays off at halftime in Chapter 5, when Youssef thinks of Simo waiting for the gap. It pays off again in the last image of Chapter 7, Simo watching younger boys with "his weight shifting from one paw to the other." Lalla's way of crossing ground "only as absence" comes back in Chapter 5 as Isklander's speed. That's a planted image reused with discipline.

**Each surface has its own voice.** The cobblestones punish, and "the absence of punishment was the only praise the stones offered" (Chapter 1). The sand forgives (Chapter 3). The turf "had no opinion" and feels "dishonest" (Chapter 5). This is the book's real structure: each surface is a teacher, and the team wins its comeback by playing all of them at once.

**The offside, as an idea.** You deny him the goal and then let Raja score straight away. Badr's "It counted… It counted" (Chapter 6) is worth more than a win would have been. It also turns the antagonist into the first person to see Youssef properly.

**Ahmed's restraint.** In Chapter 3 his "fingertips tightened — half a count — then let go," and he says "Nothing happened. That was the problem." In Chapter 7 his hand stops an inch from his son's shoulder before landing, and the weight is "Not heavier. Rearranged." He's the most alive person on the page whenever he's on it.

**The Panenka chain.** Hakimi's chip and the "something older" sound of the café (Chapter 2) set up Youssef's lifted finish, "the way his mother placed bread on the cloth," and the matching sound in Chapter 6. The chapter title earns its place.

## 3. The spine

Youssef wants to play for Morocco in 2030. Right now he wants to beat Raja. In his way: no boots, no facilities, and a drilled academy side. The arc is **missing a step**. Youssef is sure of himself on page one and sure of himself at the end. He never doubts, never fails personally, and never chooses anything that costs him. The halftime turn ("We play our mix") is a tactical insight, not an inner change.

The emotional arc actually belongs to the father and son, and its middle is missing. Chapter 7 says Youssef "held his father's hand for the first time in many years," but the book never showed us that distance. Nobody refuses anyone, nobody asks anything, and Youssef never pushes on "No one talked about what happened after."

## 4. Chapter by chapter

- **Chapter 1, Simo.** Sets up the cats, the street as a pitch, and the Phantom legend. The writing is lovely, but it's description, not scene. **Tension 2.**
- **Chapter 2, Panenka.** The phone, the 2022 memory, the vow on the roof. This is the thinnest chapter: it's mostly memory and ends on routine studying, with no turn. **Tension 2.**
- **Chapter 3, Vespa.** The birthday and the father's cryptic line give the book its first real hook, then the beach and Karim. **Tension 3.**
- **Chapter 4, Jellies.** The antagonist arrives, the crowd forms, the challenge is made. The story finally has an engine. **Tension 6.**
- **Chapter 5, Weight.** Boots, training, the match, 2-0 down to 2-2. **Tension 8.**
- **Chapter 6, Sparrow.** The run, the chip, the offside, 3-2, and Akesbi revealed. **Tension 9**, falling to 5 after the whistle.
- **Chapter 7, Phantom.** Home, the red cloth, "we cracked," the hand, Simo. **Tension 4**, and the right size.

**Where the pace goes wrong.** Three setup chapters pass before the opponent appears. In a story this short that's a lot, and Chapter 2 sags most. Chapter 5 rushes: two weeks of training happen in one paragraph, and the first half is summed up as "ten minutes chasing shadows." The 2-0 deficit never hurts long enough for the comeback to feel earned. In Chapter 6, the speed from the offside to Luca's goal to the final whistle is right.

## 5. People

**Who carries the story:** Youssef, Ahmed and Badr. Badr is the best-built secondary character: he moves from dismissive ("Not that") to bearing witness.

**Who is thin:**
- **Luca** is a cartoon. He punches a teammate and laughs.
- **Fatima and Aicha** are furniture. Aicha's crown is the only thing either of them does.
- **Omar** is loyal but has no inner life. His loose pass gives away the second goal in Chapter 5, and no one mentions it again.
- **The gardien** gets a vivid Y-shaped shirt wrinkle and "He understood," but I couldn't tell what he understood.

**Who has the wrong amount of weight:**
- **Younes** gets a whole point-of-view moment in Chapter 3 ("He watched Youssef's hunger"), then disappears from the match.
- **Bilal** is the goalkeeper in both halves, but he first appears mid-match in Chapter 5 with no introduction.
- **Ahmed** is gone from Chapter 4 through Chapter 6, and he misses the biggest day of his son's life. The page never notes that he isn't there: Youssef never scans the crowd for him. If his absence is deliberate, it's the strongest unused card in the book.

**Possibly out of character:** the medina boys accept an impossible offside call without a word of protest.

## 6. Promises

**Doors that look left open on purpose:**
- What happened to the Phantom.
- The red cloth with the gold emblem in Chapter 7.
- "But we cracked."
- Akesbi's line, "He knows you now."

These are all fine as open doors.

**Threads that look dropped:**
- **The cold water.** Ahmed's "The water is colder than it looks" and the tightened fingers at the mention of Ain Diab (Chapter 3) point to something that happened there. Nothing comes of it.
- **The Grand Stade.** Ahmed is pouring cement for the stadium where his son dreams of playing in 2030 (Chapter 1). That irony is never touched again.
- **Omar's burden.** He nods "the way a person agrees to something he will have to carry" (Chapter 2), and we never see him carry it.
- **The phone video.** The teenager recording (Chapters 4 and 5) suggests the video will spread. Chapter 7 says only that "the story was traveling."
- **Younes**, as above.

**Paid off:** Simo, the Panenka chip, the PHANTOM ELITE boots coming off, and Luca's taunt.

## 7. Theme

The book seems to say that the place you learn makes you, and that mixing places beats a single system. It also says the world won't always count your best work, but the right people see it.

**Where it says this too plainly:**
- Chapter 1: "This was the pitch. The walls were defenders…"
- Chapter 2: "They learned the same way."
- Chapter 3: "Out here the sand forgives. That's why we play different."
- Chapter 5: the halftime speech.
- The boots being called PHANTOM ELITE is the most gift-wrapped touch.

**Where it trusts the reader:** every Ahmed scene, "It counted," the F'BLADI DELMOUNI paint still wet, and the closing image.

## 8. Continuity

- **The offside (Chapter 6).** Youssef collects a loose ball inside his own half and dribbles alone. Offside can't be called on a solo run that starts in your own half, and offside needs a pass from a teammate.
- **The run's start (Chapter 6).** "Just inside his own half" doesn't fit "thirty plus meters from his own goal, sixty from the other."
- **Ahmed's age.** In Chapter 7 he was born the year ground was broken on the mosque, and seven at the last pillar. The story is set around 2025, which makes him about 39. That makes it odd that "Old" Hadj Brahim "had known Ahmed since they were boys" (Chapter 3).
- **The old man with the cane.** Hadj Brahim has a cane at the match in Chapter 5. There's also an unnamed old man with a cane on the bench at the concrete pitch in Chapter 4. And Chapter 5 lists "the two old men on the bench" separately. Is the man from Chapter 4 Hadj Brahim?
- **The mosque.** In Chapter 1 the minaret fills Youssef's window, yet in Chapter 3 he "had not been here since he was little." That might be deliberate, given that no one in the family climbs to the roof to look at it.
- **The club's name.** "Raja Club Athletic" in Chapter 4, "Raja Club Athlétique" in Chapter 5.
- **The form.** `title.md` says "A Short Story"; `chapters/README.md` says "The novella. Not finished."

## 9. The three biggest problems

**1. The father arc has no middle (Chapters 3 to 7).** The ending is built on Ahmed and Youssef. But they share one scene before it, Youssef never pursues the silence, and Ahmed's absence from the match goes unremarked. Chapter 7 is reaching for a payoff without a setup.

**2. The offside can't happen as written (Chapter 6).** This is the climax of a book dedicated "for my football brothers," and they'll be the first to see it. If you mean it as a stolen goal, the page has to show it as theft: someone protests, or someone sees the linesman. If you don't, the run needs a pass in it.

**3. Youssef doesn't change, and the start is slow (Chapters 1 to 3, and 5).** With no flaw or doubt in him, the match is the only thing at stake. Combined with three chapters of setup and a compressed training week, the story spends its words before the conflict and not inside it.

## 10. What only the author can answer

Several things on the page I couldn't settle on my own:
- What the red cloth is.
- Who "we" is in "we cracked."
- Whether something happened to Ahmed at Ain Diab.
- Whether Ahmed misses the match on purpose.
- Whether this is a finished short story or the first movement of a novella.
- In Chapter 7, whether Youssef's "The Phantom" is him naming himself or naming his father.

## After the read: what the author told me

Added after the cold read, from conversation the same evening. The cold read above is left exactly as written.

- **What it is:** The first movement of a novella, not a finished short story. It draws on his visit to Morocco two years ago.
- **The red cloth:** The Moroccan national team jersey. Ahmed was an elite national-team player.
- **"We cracked":** The national squad failed to qualify for the World Cup. This is meant to stay implicit. A background supplement is coming.
- **The mosque:** The institution that should have protected the kids and the community, and the system that turned its back on them. The repulsion is deliberate.
- **The Phantom and the boots:** Ahmed's nickname from his prime. "Phantom Elite" is Nike's real boot line, which ties the father's legacy to the gear.
- **The offside:** Meant as a corrupt call by referees who seem to favor the club. The author agrees that, as written, it's a stretch.

**The correction I owe on the offside.** I read the referee consulting the linesman and Badr's "It counted," but I didn't give them enough weight. Both do signal to an alert reader that the call was wrong and that Raja knew it. The narrower finding is that the corruption is on the page but quiet: nobody on Youssef's side reacts.

**The author's planned fix:** A wall pass. Youssef plays the ball to a medina teammate and takes the return as he runs past the defender. Then offside becomes a judgment call on when the return is played, so a wrong flag is something a reader can believe. For the call to be possible at all, the return has to be played in Raja's half. The run currently starts in Youssef's own half.
