## The lens, in one paragraph

Three of Ondaatje's habits, used on purpose. One: put an object and a motion where a feeling would be explained. Youssef's anger after the flag is cold hands and a radio in another apartment, not a sentence about anger. Two: hold back the one fact the reader wants. The cap is seen and then dropped. Three: precision, not decoration. Every image comes from things an eleven-year-old in the medina has actually seen: a puddle, bread, a curtain, a bus stop, a hose, moths. No big words were put in his head.

## Change by change

### 1. The loose ball

**Was:** "He had felt the bounce before the bounce arrived — the way Hamza on the fountain wall had always felt the ball coming. Thirty plus meters from his own goal. Sixty from the other."

**Is:** "He did not flinch at the bounce. He adjusted, one step, never two, and the ball came down onto his instep as if it had been looking for him."

**Why:** The plan says show the cats' moves and don't name the cats in the match. "Did not flinch... adjusted... one step, never two" is Simo's own sentence about Hamza, so a reader who remembers Chapter 1 hears the cat without being told. The meters are gone because the goal is no longer a long solo run. Phantom will no longer use "thirty meters" either.

### 2. The solo run becomes the wall pass

**Was:** The gap between center-back and right back, and "the way Lalla moved when she decided to cross the open ground."

**Is:** Chams-Eddine drifts left "watching the spaces between the players." Youssef gives it and goes. Chams-Eddine returns it first time. "It was the best pass anyone had made all night."

**Why:** Decided: Chams-Eddine plays the wall pass. He was beaten in the air for the first goal, so the best pass of the night is his way back. "Watching the spaces between the players" repeats Jellies' description of him, so it ties his reading to the cat without naming Simo in the match. Lalla's absence move already belongs to Isklander's equalizer in Weight. Using it twice would wear it out.

### 3. Clearly onside, shown and not stated

**Is:** "When it left his foot, Youssef was still a step behind the center-back's shoulder. He saw the shoulder in front of him. He saw the white number on the shirt."

**Why:** The flag has to be wrong beyond argument, or Badr's "It counted" means nothing. A boy can't think "I am onside" in a rules voice. He can see a number on a back in front of him. The number on the shirt is **invented**.

### 4. One more move

**Is:** "the ball drawn back under his sole, the center-back's boot sliding through nothing."

**Why:** The plan has one more move before the goal. "Sole" is correct under the vocabulary rule: this is contact and labor, not artistry.

### 5. The plant for Phantom

**Was:** "Youssef lifted the ball."

**Is:** "Youssef held the ball until the keeper's knees bent, then lifted it."

**Why:** This is the plant for "You waited for his knees." One clause, the action seen and not explained. It stays inside his old one-line paragraph, so the rhythm is still his. The bread and the curtain are exactly as he wrote them. No tea.

### 6. The defenders appeal, then the flag

**Was:** "And then the flag went up."

**Is:** "Two Raja defenders turned to the linesman with their arms up, the way defenders always do. And then the flag went up."

**Why:** The decision says the defenders appeal and the flag goes up anyway. Their reflex makes "anyway" land without the word. His "And then the flag went up" stays.

### 7. Cold and deaf (new)

**Is:** No argument. Omar's mouth, Yazid's arms, Chams-Eddine's hands on his head, all silent. "He could hear his own breath going in and out, and nothing behind it." Hot evening, cold hands.

**Why:** Decided: he doesn't protest, he goes cold and deaf. The cold hands on a hot night is the object-and-motion swap. It shows the break without saying the word anger. Omar comes first among the silent faces so that his "hold" a moment later is something we have already watched Youssef stop hearing.

### 8. The lunge (new, replaces Luca's open-play goal)

**Was:** A long ball to Badr, Luca's chest-and-drill, "His outstretched glove grazed nothing."

**Is:** A fast restart, Luca running into space, "Youssef ran after him. Not back to his place. After the ball." Omar's "Youssef — hold!" heard "the way you hear a radio in another apartment." He runs past Chams-Eddine "as if he were a post." "His leg went before he decided." A brush on the ankle. Luca rolls three times, face turned toward the referee. "It was the only fair call of the night, and it was his."

**Why:** Each piece is from the lunge decision. He chases alone to take something back. It is the first time he doesn't hear Omar. He passes a teammate who is already in position. The contact is late and slight, and Luca sells it. "His leg went before he decided" is the line the book needs: selfish, not violent. He has something to carry home, and no one has a reason to lecture him at the doorway. The radio simile and "as if he were a post" are **invented** images, kept to the size of his world. "And it was his" is the one moment the chapter lets him own it. Undo it if it feels like the narrator talking.

### 9. The free kick (new)

**Is:** Badr sets the ball "the way a man waits for a bus." Luca is "up now and not limping." "Bilal dove as if reaching across sand. His fingertips found it. / It went in anyway."

**Why:** Luca gets the show and Badr the cold execution, as decided. "Not limping" shows the dive without anyone saying so. "Dove as if reaching across sand" is **his** line, moved from the old goal to the free kick, as the decision says. The fingertips replace "grazed nothing." "It went in anyway" stands alone so the quality of the strike and Bilal's effort both sit in the white space.

### 10. Kept as written

"3-2." "The Raja faithful cheered. The quietness was louder." The last minute. The call to prayer paragraph, complete, including Stade Mohammed V. "The final whistle came." The ragged circle and Omar's hand. "The turf burned, and he stood on it anyway." Hadj Brahim's cane and the families' applause. Luca's flag wave. Badr not moving.

### 11. Karim's jellies moved

**Was:** In the ragged circle: "Karim and the coastal boys had traded their cleats for jellies and white socks."

**Is:** The last paragraph of the chapter.

**Why:** The plan puts the jellies last. See change 15.

### 12. The coastal stranger (new)

**Is:** "From the bleachers, a voice said it once. / 'Qit sihri.' / Low, the way it had started on the concrete. Nobody joined in." A face, alone, "brown and creased the way wind and salt leave a face." A family stands to go, and the place is empty.

**Why:** All of this is locked: once, nobody joins, the face lost in the crowd. Wind and salt follow canon (weathered, coastal, no fish imagery, no cane). The family standing up is the **invented** way the face gets lost.

### 13. A beat apart (new)

**Is:** "The floodlights had moths in them now. Someone on the Raja side was coiling a hose."

**Why:** The plan keeps the stranger a beat apart from Hadj Brahim's cane, so the two old men don't blur. Two plain things in the night are the beat. Both are **invented**. Ondaatje uses small working objects like this to let time pass.

### 14. Badr: "I know" and the smile cut

**Was:** "'I know,' Youssef said. He smiled." Then Badr looked at the bare feet and the boots hanging from his fingers.

**Is:** "Youssef looked at the PHANTOM ELITE boots, tied together by their laces and hanging from his fingers." Badr "looked at Youssef's bare feet."

**Why:** Decided: cut "I know" and the smile. He looks at the boots instead. The boots moved from Badr's look to Youssef's, so the look became his answer. Badr keeps the bare feet ("feet" is the world's view of the body, correct for an outsider's eye). Everything else in the dialogue, from "Where did you learn?" to "He knows you now," is exactly as he wrote it.

### 15. Hadj Brahim's glance and the cap (new)

**Is:** Brahim comes down the bleachers "the cane first," stops, and looks back past the fence toward the public park. Youssef looks where he looks. "Under the last lamp on the path, a man in a work cap stood with his hands at his sides. The cap was grey, the grey of cement dust. Then the man turned toward the road, and Youssef looked down at the boots in his hand."

**Why:** The plan's order puts the glance and the cap here, after Badr. Brahim's look gives Youssef's eye a reason to travel. Nothing explains how Ahmed came. Youssef knows cement dust from his father's knuckles, but he doesn't connect it, and looking down at the boots is him not looking twice. "Hands at his sides" is **invented**. It quietly carries the hands thread into Phantom. The last lamp and the road are **invented**.

### 16. The ending: Karim (new)

**Is:** Karim knocks his cleats together "the way you knock out sand, though there was no sand," puts on his jellies, waits. "Karim's jellies printing soft shapes in the turf, Youssef's bare soles leaving nothing at all."

**Why:** Karim trading cleats for jellies is locked, and it is the right last image: the coast going home as the coast. Knocking out sand that isn't there is a beach boy's habit on someone else's grass. It is **invented**. Bare soles leaving nothing is the door left open. It echoes a father who was "never where you expected" without saying so. This is the most Ondaatje line in the chapter, so it is the first one to undo if it feels too pointed.

## Voice-age check

Lines most at risk of sounding older than twelve: "hurry creates space" (his line, narrator's voice, kept); "without distinguishing between them" (his line, kept); "It was the only fair call of the night, and it was his" (mine; watch it). Nothing else in the new prose uses a word or idea a medina boy wouldn't have.
