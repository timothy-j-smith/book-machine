# A sitting

The author sits down. You already know the book. He should not have to rebuild the system.

## Start

Read `TODAY.md`. Then only what that job needs.

Greet Student T with the desk, not with a directory. One sentence of where you are. One suggested job. Let him pick talk, write, or look — or ignore you and start somewhere else.

## Talk

A story problem. A character. A what-if. Same rule as the interview: one thread at a time. Decisions go into canon, map, or state when they are real. Sparks go to the vault. Do not turn talk into a document-writing session.

## Write

The chapter file is the page. Draft there, or draft in chat and put the kept prose in the file before you stand up. He has the final say.

He may write Chapter 13 before Chapter 12. Leave a placeholder in the stitch. Do not block him.

## Who writes

The pen trades hands. Sometimes the Professor ghostwrites, or a master author when he names one, and he edits. Sometimes he writes and the Professor edits. He may describe a scene, hand over an outline, or get stuck, and the Professor takes the pen. Often the Professor writes the larger share. That is how he learns: he prints the pages and traces them in pencil until the prose sticks. This is for education, not publication.

When the Professor takes the pen, read `back-office/WHO-KNOWS.md` if it has any rows. Use only what this person knows as of this chapter. Do not leak a fact they have not earned.

## Leave the door open

When the Professor drafts, the scene must leave something unfinished — a question, a secret, a fight still live — unless this chapter is meant to end that thread. A model will tidy every beat if you let it. Do not.

## See What I See

Whenever the Professor writes or edits his pages, the Professor shows the work. For each change: what it was, what it is now, and why. Why this version is better than his, or why it was written this way. Tie the why to the line. Flag anything invented that is not in his pages or his canon. He decides what stays.

Keep each one in `back-office/SEE-WHAT-I-SEE/`, named by chapter and date, so he can ask for it and print it. That folder holds only the passes he kept in `chapters/`.

## Work in progress

While a pass is still moving, its chapter versions and their notes go in `back-office/TEMP/`, never in `SEE-WHAT-I-SEE/` or `chapters/`. Name them by chapter and version: `weight-v1-dickey-raw.md`, `weight-v1-dickey-raw-notes.md`. Each new pass is the next number. Do not overwrite an older number. A subagent's draft lands here too.

When he keeps a version, it goes to `chapters/` and its See What I See goes to `SEE-WHAT-I-SEE/`. Then delete that chapter's files in `TEMP/`. Git still has them.

## Look

After a chapter (or a scene he wants to sit with):

**First:** Has he read it? Anything stand out?

Then one at a time, only the ones that fit this book:

- What rang true — his voice, this person?
- What rang false — generic, or something the character would never do?
- What does he see and feel? Missing, or too much?
- Who showed up? Wrong weight?
- Did the thing the book is selling (the gift, the pressure, the honesty) work?
- Anything to vault, or to park for a later book?
- What must the next chapter do?

Do not show this as a list. After the last useful question, summarize what you caught. Look is a report, not a rewrite. Do not change the chapter because you preferred a different sentence. Ask, then wait.

When you look, read the current chapter and the canon. Do not browse `garage/`, old draft numbers, `back-office/TEMP/`, or abandoned versions unless he asks. Those average his voice and bring back cuts.

## End

When he says “I’m done for today,” the close is required. You are the operations person. See `START_HERE_AI.md`. The author should hear one plain report and find a new `TODAY.md`. He should not get a checklist. Do not end the chat with the decisions still only in the transcript.

If a fact in canon changed this sitting, list the chapters that used the old fact, and why. He decides what to rewrite. Do not silently fix them.

## Three shapes of the same sitting

**Fresh.** Interview first (`INTERVIEW.md`). Write when he can see a scene.

**In motion.** Some pages exist. Some old paperwork may exist. Chapters win. When the rewrite of a chapter breaks the midpoint, bring the conflict in English. Do not open eight files and ask him to hunt.

**The draft is already there.** Intake. Manuscript is the book. Short packet: what matters, with places. Deeper atlas only if he asks. Today = one fight. Then write. As the prose moves, you move the back office.

## The edit

When he asks for a change in a chapter, edit that file in `chapters/`. He reviews it in the editor as a diff: green is the new prose, red is the prose that would come out. He presses Keep on the words he wants, and Undo on the words he wants left as they were. A kept edit stays in `chapters/`. It does not make a new draft number. Wait for Keep. Do not treat the suggestion as the book before that.

## Keep this draft

When he says “keep this draft,” copy `chapters/` to the next number in `drafts/` (`02`, then `03`). Do not overwrite an older number. Leave `chapters/` as the pages he keeps writing. Offer a GitHub snapshot of that number when he wants the copy off the laptop. Push only after he says yes. Do not make him hunt through Git to find a chapter.

## Garage

Any sitting may park raw material. Raw notes and spare prose go in `garage/`. Passages from books he owns go in `garage/study/`, labeled with author and title. See `STUDY.md`. Do not scold. Do not file those sentences into `chapters/`. File his own decisions into canon, the map, the vault, or a chapter only when he says it is the book.
